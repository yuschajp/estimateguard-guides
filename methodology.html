Methodology: how we build these numbers

What these benchmarks are, what they aren't, and why we show our work.

What the numbers are

Every price range on this site is compiled from published cost guides — sources like Angi, HomeAdvisor, HomeGuide, and regional contractor cost guides. Each guide page lists the sources its figures came from, and the date those figures were published.

What the numbers are not

They are not observed contractor transactions. EstimateGuard has not yet collected real invoices from real jobs, so every benchmark on this site has an observed sample size of zero. We'd rather tell you that plainly than let you assume these are market measurements.

How to use them

  • Planning: budgeting a project before you call anyone.
  • Sanity-checking: comparing contractor quotes against the published range for your metro.
  • Negotiating: asking informed questions when a quote lands far outside the range.

They are not quotes, not guarantees, and not a substitute for getting multiple bids from licensed contractors in your area.

What "not published" means

When a value is missing, it means the cost guides we compiled didn't publish one — not that the cost is zero, and not that we filled it in. We never interpolate or invent figures.

Coverage

Benchmarks are metro-level, not ZIP-level: one set of ranges per city. Costs within a metro vary by neighborhood, access, and project specifics.

Updates

Each guide page shows the publication date of its underlying figures. As we begin observing real contractor transactions through our estimate checker, we will say so — and the methodology on every affected page will change to reflect it.
